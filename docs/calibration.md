# Analog calibration

There are sixteen independent channel/range profiles, each a monotonic raw ADC -> true voltage table of 2..32 points and a version. The app stores schema version, device UID, profiles and channel/bitrate preferences in `%LOCALAPPDATA%/CarSniffer/calibration-<uid>.json`. It uploads profiles after connecting or observing a new MCU boot. Foreign device IDs and duplicate/non-monotonic tables are rejected. A newly edited profile is saved only after an MCU acknowledgement. MCU profiles are RAM-only; without an app upload values are nominal and flagged uncalibrated. Saved bitrate selectors still require explicit Apply to configure CAN; bridge/capture never resume automatically.

Use the Calibration dialog to select channel/range, collect raw, enter a stable external-voltmeter reading, add a DC point, review/edit the table and plot, then upload/save. Source voltage must already be safe before switching ranges. Collection lasts 0.2..10 s; do not change channel/range during it. DC requires at least 160 samples, std <=3 ADC counts, and no samples <=2 or >=4093. Close/duplicate raw breakpoints (less than two counts) and non-increasing voltage are rejected. Use several points, including near zero and near the intended upper bound without saturation; the strict clipping guard means a truly zero-ADC point cannot be captured automatically and needs a justified manually entered anchor.

The ADC's raw resolution is 12 bits; arithmetic oversampling does not promise extra effective resolution. Raw mean/std are diagnostics. Each individual raw sample is converted through the nonlinear table before averaging voltage. Outside the calibrated raw interval the value is NaN/uncalibrated, with no extrapolation. Clipping is tested before averaging. The 15 V UI range still uses nominal divisor factor 5 and would nominally reach 16.5 V at full scale; selected-range exceedance is flagged rather than silently clamped.

## Known sine identification

Collect the raw selected channel at 1600 samples/s (not the 100 Hz mean). Enter frequency <=20 Hz, amplitude definition (peak / peak-to-peak / RMS) and independently known DC offset. The whole waveform must fit inside 0..3.3 V or 0..15 V. Choose a duration containing at least five full periods (up to 10 s). A single amplitude without known offset/reference is insufficient and is not supported as nonlinear identification.

`sine_profile` estimates phase from the fundamental of the measured raw waveform, trims incomplete periods, constructs the known reference sine, then bins the monotonic raw axis and checks ascending versus descending branches. A constant source/ADC delay is absorbed into phase. Both branches require coverage in all bins; clipping, irregular/gapped timing, insufficient amplitude coverage, non-monotonic fit, branch disagreement or excessive residual noise reject the result. The resulting 16-point table covers only the verified interior interval; its edges are shown and no extrapolation is performed. Tests use a known quadratic static characteristic, unknown phase/delay, noise and clipping.

This identifies a *static, repeatable, monotonic* characteristic under the supplied reference assumptions. It cannot independently verify the generator amplitude, offset, frequency or general dynamic nonlinearity. A phase fit can absorb some dynamic error; branch checks do not prove absence of hysteresis. If rejection occurs, keep/use DC calibration and measure additional DC anchors; the app never replaces it with an amplitude-only coefficient or an unreliable table. Sine calibration is not yet validated on the physical divider.

## Hardware conditions still open

D1/D2 actual types, clamp behavior, source impedance, temperature drift and settling need measurement. Firmware's 480-cycle sample time and 20 ms settling are explicit provisional parameters. Reset gives Hi-Z/unity range until firmware initializes OD outputs low. This makes direct high-voltage input during startup unsafe until external protection/startup circuitry is verified. The prototype is not qualified for vehicle transients.
